# Solar Client Knowledge

## Who the client is
Amit Mathur, from Madhya Pradesh. He is a solar products dealer. He is a supplier and a middleman and he also deals directly with manufacturers. He buys direct from the manufacturer and sells direct to the customer. He has his own production house. He sells mostly Reliance solar products and has connections with Reliance.

## Scope: Madhya Pradesh only
- Target all major MP cities: Indore, Bhopal, Jabalpur, Gwalior, Ujjain, Sagar, Dewas, Satna, Ratlam, Rewa.
- Target rural areas across all 55 MP districts, down to Gram Panchayat level.
- Segments: B2B and B2C, urban and rural. Pitch everywhere, capture leads everywhere.
- MP has about 23,000 Gram Panchayats and about 72 percent of the state lives in rural areas. The village market is the biggest market.

## Business model
- Supplier: holds local stock of panels, inverters and structures.
- Middleman: connects buyers to manufacturers when a direct deal is better.
- Direct dealer: buys from the manufacturer and sells straight to the customer, cutting the chain.
- Production house: mounting structures and fabrication in house, so install quality is in his control.

## What he sells
- Rooftop solar systems for homes, 1 kW to 10 kW, most common 3 kW and 5 kW.
- Commercial and industrial systems for factories, warehouses, hotels, hospitals and institutions.
- Solar pumps and farm systems.
- Reliance solar panels, made at the Jamnagar giga factory.
- Installation, net meter filing and DISCOM paperwork.
- After-sales service and a 5-year replacement warranty (coverage to be confirmed in writing by the client).

## What he is NOT
- He is not Reliance. Never claim he is Reliance, an authorised Reliance partner or an official dealer unless he confirms that status in writing.
- He is not a government agency and cannot grant subsidies. He can only help a buyer apply through the official portal.

## Buyer types
- Homeowners who want lower electricity bills.
- Housing societies and RWAs for common facilities.
- Factories and warehouses with large roofs.
- Farms needing solar pumps.
- Schools, colleges, clinics and hospitals.
- Gram Panchayats and Sarpanchs: the Sarpanch is the village influencer. One Sarpanch word can move a whole village to install solar.

## The Sarpanch strategy (user proven play)
- Pitch the Sarpanch of a Gram Panchayat on village solar and the subsidy.
- Get the Sarpanch mobile number.
- Message the Sarpanch daily with offers through Google Messages for web.
- The Sarpanch shares the offer with villagers. When the Sarpanch says install, the maximum villagers install at home.
- Proof case from the user: he pitched one Sarpanch once and 35 homes in that village installed solar panels.
- Sarpanch outreach is a first-class pipeline, not a side note. Target 10 Sarpanch pitches a day, scale to 20.

## SMS channel: Google Messages for web
- Open messages.google.com/web on the computer.
- On the Android phone open Google Messages, tap profile, then Device pairing, and scan the QR code. Google is phasing out QR pairing in favour of Google account sign-in, so sign-in may be offered instead.
- The phone must stay on and connected to mobile data or Wi-Fi. SMS sends through the phone carrier, from Amit real number.
- This is manual 1-to-1 messaging, not a bulk gateway. One offer per day per Sarpanch, maximum. Personalise every message with the village name and the offer. Stop at once if they opt out.
- No SMS is sent by the automation. The runner only drafts and tracks. A human sends from the web UI.

## Compliance flag: TRAI DLT (read only, no status claimed)
- Bulk commercial SMS in India needs DLT registration under TRAI TCCCPR 2018: Principal Entity ID, a 6-character header with a -P, -S, -T or -G category suffix, a registered content template, and a consent template. Unregistered bulk SMS is blocked by operators.
- No DLT registration status is claimed for Amit. The Sarpanch SMS leg is manual 1-to-1 messaging from his own phone, not bulk gateway traffic.
- Even so, keep volume human, message only people who were pitched or asked, honour opt-outs at once. If volume grows, move to a DLT-registered bulk SMS route.

## Engagement rules (apply to every pipeline)
- If a lead looks like a hot buyer, ask qualifying questions: roof size and type, monthly electricity bill, location (city or village), timeline, budget range.
- Even if a lead does NOT look like a buyer, still ask questions, engage them, and try to convert. Never drop a non-obvious lead without engaging first.
- Ask questions first, pitch second. Questions turn a cold lead into a warm one.

## Voice
- Warm Indian professional, plain words, no hype, no em dashes.
- Show the buyer their exact problem first, then the fix, then the offer.
- Always end a pitch or comment with a DM call to action.
- Never share a phone number or price in a public reply unless the buyer asks for it.
- For Sarpanch messages: short, respectful, Hindi or Hinglish is fine, village name in every message.
